# Nonsuch maths realism review

Checked 8 November 2026. This review covers maths, not English preparation.

## Sources found

- [Official SET maths samples, Nonsuch](https://www.nonsuchschool.org/_site/data/files/information/admissions/set2027/96D936786A72865A82CBAF7C7E2ECC0A.pdf): the stage-one multiple-choice benchmark. A short selection, not a full paper.
- [Official NWSSEE maths samples and worked answers, Wallington](https://www.wallingtongirls.org.uk/_site/data/files/admissions/admissions-2025/012EDD9C6D576D0BF137A7D0B5213CED.pdf), linked from [the school's older second-stage page](https://www.wallingtongirls.org.uk/page/?pid=198&title=NONSUCH+AND+WALLINGTON+SECOND+STAGE+ENTRANCE+EXAMINATION+%28NWSSEE%29): an official selection for the joint Nonsuch and Wallington exam. Its answer-sheet introduction says the questions came from past entrance tests and that difficulty is distributed throughout the paper. It includes routine calculation and problems combining familiar methods. This is historical evidence, not a statement of the next paper's contents.
- [Current Nonsuch second-stage page](https://www.nonsuchschool.org/page/?pid=300&title=Second+Stage+Entrance+Examination+(NWSSEE)) and [2027-entry FAQ](https://www.nonsuchschool.org/_site/data/files/information/admissions/set2027/A591D2C1E6F8BD03BBA671E854E30FF5.pdf): the current format and conditions. The omission of sample links from this newer FAQ does not mean older official samples do not exist.
- [Revision Library NWSSEE mocks](https://www.revisionlibrary.co.uk/eleven-plus/mock-papers/nonsuch-wallington-girls-second-stage) and [SET maths mocks](https://www.revisionlibrary.co.uk/eleven-plus/mock-papers/sutton-set/stage-1/maths): printable third-party practice. The publisher identifies these as original reconstructions. They are optional practice resources, not evidence of actual past paper difficulty or school endorsement.

No complete school-released Nonsuch past entrance paper or past school mock was verified. The official historical NWSSEE selection is the strongest past-test evidence found. We link the originals and write our own questions; no source questions are reproduced in the bank.

## Assessment and changes

Before this review the written bank contained 42 difficulty-1 exercises out of 150, and the SET bank contained 23 out of 150. Several legacy families repeated the same straightforward method with different numbers. Much of the newer bank already required multiple steps, so replacing the whole bank or making every item a stretch problem would not reflect the published samples.

The review strengthens 26 legacy written questions and 12 SET questions. Examples include choosing whole cartons before computing a surplus, comparing complete orders with different offers, using percentages after a group changes, finding both sides of an unknown-number relationship, and checking boarding constraints before choosing a train. Every replacement has updated hints, a worked solution and an arithmetic proof; SET distractors describe specific wrong calculations.

After the review there are 22 difficulty-1 written questions and 11 difficulty-1 SET questions. These are author judgements, not measured equivalence to the real entrance exam. Oral warm-ups remain easier. The six existing mock papers retain their question IDs and order, equal topic quotas, similar difficulty mixes and distributed harder questions. Question counts and the default 45-minute duration are practice design choices, not an official paper blueprint. Exact current difficulty, topic weighting and pass prediction cannot be established from short sample selections.

## Harder practice requested on 9 November

The follow-up strengthens another 45 questions: all 33 remaining written warm-ups, plus four existing questions in each NWSSEE mock. That is 34 written-answer changes and 11 SET changes, taking the cumulative total to 83 replacements (60 written-answer and 23 SET).

The added demand comes from combining KS2 methods: reverse currency exchanges, interpreting a chart before recovering a total, undoing a translation, combining reflections with translations, reversing successive spending or price changes, and checking whether a cutting or seating arrangement is feasible. For example, a former one-step flour conversion now requires scaling a recipe to a different number of rolls before converting and finding what remains. Revised questions have their own progressive hints and worked solutions. No source questions have been copied.

All 300 written questions are now rated 2 or 3. This is intentionally a harder practice bank, informed by the sample question families and reasoning demands. It should not be presented as a measured reproduction of the official papers' difficulty distribution. The original oral bank remains available for warm-ups. Paper membership, order, topic balance and unseen reservations are unchanged.
